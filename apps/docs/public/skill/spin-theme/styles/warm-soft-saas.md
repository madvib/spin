# Warm Soft-SaaS

The product is not trying to look expensive. It is trying to look like something
a person would be happy to open on a Tuesday morning, and the warmth is what
does that work.

This is the direction for the software people pay for without being sold to: a
form, a list, a small dashboard, a settings page. It is the style that ages
best, because nothing in it can go out of fashion.

## The type voice

One friendly family, a geometric or a humanist sans, used everywhere. The weight
does the work: one step above the body for a heading, nothing more. Nothing is
set in capitals except the smallest overline labels, which are tracked open and
quiet.

The scale is comfortable rather than dramatic. The body is a size a person can
read for an hour, the display step exists for a page head and is a noticeable
but not theatrical jump, and the labels are small enough to disappear.

The type is quiet on purpose. In this style the material is the loud part.

## The palette direction

Warm neutrals. Cream, sand, taupe, a paper that has a temperature, at several
lightnesses. The ramp is tinted, not grey, and the tint is the style's first
decision: a warm grey ramp with a cool accent is a different product from a warm
one.

One soft accent, a teal, a coral, a violet, chosen for being saturated but not
loud. It fills the primary action and one other thing, and it is never a
hairline, because a hairline of a soft colour reads as a mistake.

The status colours are soft relatives of the accent rather than separate hues,
so nothing on the page shouts.

## The shape

Soft, and unmistakably so. A radius ladder that runs from eight pixels to
sixteen as the surface grows, pills for the primary action and for tags, and a
small control that is round enough to be obviously friendly.

The radius ladder has to have a shape of its own here. A theme with one radius
of eight everywhere is not warm, it is just small.

## The depth

Soft ambient shadows, wide and low in opacity, so a raised surface reads as
floating a little above the page, and a lighter surface for the raised one
beside it. Never a hard offset: a hard offset is a different style's language
and it reads as an error in a soft theme.

The shadow is quiet enough to be felt rather than seen.

## The motion

Quick and friendly, in the one-hundred-and-fifty to two-hundred-and-fifty
millisecond region, with a gentle ease-out. A hover lifts a couple of pixels and
settles. A press pushes in slightly and comes back. A spring may be used, but
one that barely shows: the motion should feel like something with a little
weight, not like a toy.

## The density

Comfortable. Generous padding inside cards, a row height of forty to forty-eight
pixels, a form that can hold its fields without the page feeling crowded, and a
page that is never more than one thing per screen.

## The decisions that make it recognisable

1. **A warm, tinted neutral ramp instead of a grey one.** The temperature is the
   style. Change it to cool grey and the theme is still the same product, but
   nobody would call it warm.
2. **A radius ladder that is unmistakably soft, with pills on the primary
   action.**
3. **One soft accent, spent on fills rather than on hairlines,** so the colour
   reads as an action and not as a mistake.

## How it drifts into the house style

It drifts when the neutrals go cold, because warmth was the whole temperature of
the style. It drifts when the radius ladder collapses to four pixels, because a
soft theme with small corners is a serious theme wearing a friendly label. And
it drifts when the accent is demoted to a hairline colour, because a soft
product with a monochrome interface has become a different product.

The tell is the tint. Warm neutrals and a cool accent never live together here.
