# High-End Agency

The work is the content. Everything else is a frame for it, and a good frame is
invisible until someone notices how well it is made.

This is the direction for a product whose users arrive from a referral, look
once, and decide. It is expensive to fake and cheap to keep: the whole style is
a small palette, one confident type pairing, and motion that arrives rather than
snaps.

## The type voice

A display face with presence sets the statement, and it is set once. A high
contrast serif or a tight grotesque are both right; what is wrong is a display
face that is only a size above the body. The display step is a jump: the
statement is set several times larger than the body, and it is set in the
display face, so the jump is felt twice.

Everything else is one neutral sans, quiet and even. A mono is worth having for
the small technical facts: a date, a project code, a number that has to line up.
Tracking is tight at display size and open on the small labels. The body is not
set at a large size; the display is not set small.

## The palette direction

Almost nothing, and that is the discipline. Near-black surfaces that are not
black, around a warm or a cool charcoal rather than pure `#000`, so the surface
has a temperature. Ink that is not pure white, a shade or two down, so it does
not glare on a dark ground.

One accent, and it is used on a rule, on a single word, or on one small mark. It
is not a button colour and it is not a background. If the accent appears twice
on a screen, it is already too much.

The neutrals do the work: the surfaces, the hairlines, the ink, and the muted
ink. Everything else is one colour.

## The shape

Large radii on the surfaces that float, in the region of sixteen to twenty-four
pixels, so every surface reads as a pebble rather than a rectangle. Hairlines at
one pixel, in an ink that is barely there.

Pills for the primary action only. A pill is a claim, and one claim per theme is
enough.

## The depth

Soft, wide, low opacity shadows that lift a card a few millimetres off the page,
paired with the hairline so the card has an edge as well as a distance. The
shadow is the style's one effect, and it is used once: on the surfaces that
float, and on nothing else.

The page itself does not float. It is the ground, and everything on it is either
the ground or a thing hovering above it.

## The motion

Slow and eased out. Three hundred and fifty to seven hundred milliseconds, with
a long settle and no overshoot. The page arrives; it does not spring.

Motion is spent on entrances, not on state changes. A hover is quick and nearly
invisible, because a hover that takes half a second is a hover that was not
designed.

## The density

Airy, and deliberately so. Wide margins, one idea per screen, a content column
narrow enough to read comfortably and a lot of nothing around it. The nothing is
the point: it is what makes the one thing on the screen look expensive.

## The decisions that make it recognisable

1. **A near-black canvas with off-white type and exactly one accent.** The
   accent never becomes a fill.
2. **A large radius ladder, so every surface is a pebble.** This is the
   signature of the style and the first thing that goes when it drifts.
3. **Motion that is slow and settles.** Nothing bounces, nothing springs,
   nothing arrives in a hurry.

## How it drifts into the house style

It drifts the moment a second accent appears, because a second accent is the
beginning of a palette. It drifts when the radius collapses to the page's
radius, because the pebbles become rectangles and the style becomes "a dark
theme". And it drifts when the motion speeds up: a
one-hundred-and-fifty-millisecond transition with a large radius and a
near-black canvas is a dashboard, not an agency.

The tell is the accent. If the accent has become the button's fill, the style is
gone.
