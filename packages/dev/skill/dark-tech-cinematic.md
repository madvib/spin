# Dark-Tech / Cinematic

The screen is the only light source in the room, and the interface is built for
that. Dark does not mean inverted: the surfaces are picked for a dim room, the
accent is the only thing that glows, and everything that is not the accent is
quiet enough to disappear.

This is the direction for software that is watched rather than read: a dashboard
that stays open on a second monitor, a live feed, an editor, a player. It is the
direction for a product whose users already know that dark is the right answer,
and it should serve one mode well rather than two modes poorly.

## The type voice

A technical sans or a geometric grotesque carries the interface. A mono carries
the values that stream: the numbers, the identifiers, the log lines, the
timings. The two are chosen to be distinguishable at a glance, because one is a
label and the other is a reading.

Hierarchy comes from weight and size. Nothing is decorative: no italics for
emphasis, no small caps for elegance, no second display face unless the product
has a marketing surface of its own, which this style handles better than most.

## The palette direction

A near-black ramp that is not black. The darkest surface sits just above black,
around `#08090b`, and the lightest is a panel grey a few steps up. Every step
carries a little blue or a little violet, because a neutral black reads as dead
and a tinted black reads as a screen.

Ink at ninety percent white rather than pure white, so the text does not flare.
One accent at high chroma, a cyan, a violet, an acid green, spent on the live
value, the focus ring, and one small indicator. The status colours are the
accent's siblings rather than separate hues, so nothing on the screen looks like
it was imported from another theme.

## The shape

Two kinds of radius and no more. Medium radii, six to ten pixels, on the panels
and cards, and pills on the small controls and the tags. Mixing them is allowed
and is part of the style; mixing in a third is not.

Hairlines at eight to twelve percent white are the separators, and they are
where the depth comes from on the flat surfaces.

## The depth

Light comes from two places only: the accent, which glows, and the hairline,
which catches the edge of a surface. Every panel gets a soft ambient shadow so
it sits above the page, and nothing else does.

Glow is the style's one effect. It is used on the accent, in one place, at one
strength. A glow on every card turns the screen into a light show and the accent
into decoration.

## The motion

The accent moves and the rest does not. A state change is quick, in the
one-hundred-and-twenty to two-hundred-millisecond region. A live indicator
pulses slowly, over two to four seconds, and that pulse is the only thing on the
screen that loops. A shimmer is for values that genuinely stream, and not for a
value that is merely loading.

## The density

Dense. A base of thirteen or fourteen pixels, rows of twenty-eight to
thirty-two, tabular figures everywhere, and enough data on the screen that the
person reading it never scrolls to find the number they wanted.

## The decisions that make it recognisable

1. **A near-black ramp with a single high-chroma accent.** One accent, and it is
   the only saturated thing on the screen.
2. **Light comes from the accent and from hairlines,** never from a shadow on
   everything. The panels float; the fields do not.
3. **A mono for the values and a sans for the labels,** so a reader can tell a
   measurement from a description without reading it.

## How it drifts into the house style

It drifts when the black warms into a charcoal, because a warm dark is a
different product: the screen stops reading as a screen. It drifts when a second
neon colour joins the accent, because two accents mean the first one means
nothing. And it drifts when the shadows come back on every card, because then
the depth is furniture rather than light.

The tell is the glow. Two glows, and the screen is a poster.
